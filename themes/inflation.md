# Inflation

## Thesis

An energy-led inflation impulse is colliding with cores that had been cooling. Brent surged ~30% month-on-month toward $100/bbl on Red Sea/Middle East supply risk, reviving hike talk at the Fed, ECB and BOJ — even though the most recent core prints (Eurozone June core 2.4%, Malaysia June CPI 1.9%) were still trending down. The central tension for H2 2026 is whether the energy shock passes through to core/services inflation or proves transitory.

## Supporting Evidence

Brent crude ~$97-100/bbl intraday, +~30% m/m (from ~$71.57 on 1 Jul); ECB's Lagarde: 'the full inflationary impact of the energy shock has yet to play out' (23 Jul); Fed 2026 hike odds ~71% per Polymarket (via Yahoo Finance).

## Contradicting Evidence

Eurozone June HICP core at 2.4% (down from 2.6%); Malaysia June CPI at 1.9% (transport-led moderation) — both pre-shock but still the latest confirmed core reads.

## Observation 2026-08-03 — headline and core decouple

The July HICP/CPI prints already in the data (Eurozone core 2.5% with energy **+10.0% y/y**, Germany 2.8%, Spain 3.5%) captured the war premium at its peak. The weekend broke that premium: Brent from a $100 test to ~$84 live, OPEC+ completing the 1.65m b/d unwind with a Q4 pause, Henry Hub −17% across July, CBOT wheat −24.25c Friday. **Base effects will now work the other way into the August and September prints** (Analyst inference, medium confidence — no forecaster's revised path was sourced this run).

Against that, the core/wage impulse is intact and is what the Fed's three dissenters cite: **US Q2 ECI +0.9% q/q vs +0.8% expected**, with real private wages −0.4% y/y. Thursday 6 Aug's Q2 productivity and unit labour costs is the direct follow-through and is the week's most underrated print for this theme.

The counterweight to a clean disinflation read is the **Russian product-crack channel**: Ufa (>23mt/yr), Rosneft's Saratov (~7mt/yr), Nizhnekamsk and Ryazan are all impaired with Russian runs at 2005 lows, while crude falls. Diesel is where a falling crude price and a tightening product supply collide, and **no diesel-crack print was sourced this run** — a named gap, not an absence.
