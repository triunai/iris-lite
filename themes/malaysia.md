# Malaysia

## Thesis

As a net oil/LNG exporter, Malaysia sits on the relative-beneficiary side of the current energy shock: the ringgit firmed and government revenue gets a direct lift from higher oil prices. But under the surface, Bursa breadth was already negative (510 decliners vs. 319 gainers) even as the KLCI itself was roughly flat — a soft undertone worth watching if the global risk-off backdrop persists, since plantations and some non-energy names were already under pressure.

## Supporting Evidence

USDMYR firmed to 4.0850/0890 (from 4.0870/0910) at the 6pm close, 23 Jul, on oil-linked support; Dep. Finance Minister Liew Chin Tong: ~RM300m in extra government revenue per +US$1/bbl; KLCI 1,711.51 at midday (+0.14 pt vs. Wed's 1,711.37 close), Energy Index +1.45 pts, Tenaga +12 sen.
- **2026-08-04 (3 Aug):** FBM KLCI closed **1,725.73 (+0.83, +0.05%)** - flat on a day Brent fell ~5% and North Asian equities fell 2-5%. Breadth negative (609 losers / 490 gainers) on 2.73bn shares / RM2.33bn, down ~35% by value from Friday. Technology +0.54% and financials +0.23% offset energy. Ringgit weakened to **4.0935/0975**. **The two oil channels separated cleanly for the first time: the fiscal/subsidy-relief channel held the index, the export-receipts channel hit the currency.** No Negeri Sembilan election commentary appeared in any sourced market report - the political discount did not visibly price. *(MCI/Bernama 3 Aug 20:12; The Star 3 Aug 19:19 MYT and 12:53 MYT)*

## Contradicting Evidence

Bursa breadth was negative (510 decliners vs. 319 gainers) despite the flat headline index; plantations were notably weak (United Plantations -84 sen, Plantation Index -73.7 pts).

## Observation 2026-08-03 — a political defeat and a fiscal windfall arriving the same weekend

BN+PN = **25 seats, a two-thirds majority**. PH fell from 17 (2023) to 11; BN rose 14→18; PN 5→7. **DAP secretary-general and Transport Minister Anthony Loke lost Chennah by 533 votes** (5,060 vs MCA's Siow Kong Choon 5,593), a seat held since 2013; Chennah's electorate is 47.8% Malay. Caretaker Menteri Besar **Aminuddin Harun lost Linggi** after vacating the safer Sikamat. Foreign Minister Mohamad Hasan retained Rantau. PKR lost 16 of 18 seats contested.

**Turnout conflict, unresolved:** EC reported 65.38% as at 4pm (562,133 of 859,760); Bloomberg reported "roughly 73%" final. No official EC final figure sourced; BERNAMA's own page gives the electorate as both 889,490 and (implicitly) 859,760. Popular vote shares unsourced.

**Government formation:** Zahid Hamidi (1 Aug 22:20 MYT) confirmed BN and PN will hold follow-up discussions to form the state administration "based on an understanding between BN and PN," made **no reference to the federal unity government**, and named no MB. PN's Hamzah had pre-committed to leaving the MB pick to BN. No MB named as at this run.

**Read (Analyst inference, medium confidence, sourced to named analysts):** Adib Zalkapli (Viewfinder, via Bloomberg/The Edge 2 Aug) judges BN unlikely to resign federally to force a general election, since Anwar depends on BN to hold the coalition together — but flags political uncertainty, possible ringgit weakness, DAP reconsidering cabinet positions, and reduced odds of a second Anwar term. Sivamurugan Pandian (USM): "a serious warning, but not yet a death sentence for PH." Azmi Hassan: PH bled Malay votes to both BN and PN. GE16 is due by February 2028. This is the **third consecutive state setback** — Sabah (Nov 2025, one seat), Johor (11 Jul 2026), now Negeri Sembilan.

**The offsetting fiscal channel (this is the more important macro fact):** BIMB Securities (via The Edge, 28 Jul) estimated 2026 fuel subsidies at **RM40bn vs RM21.6bn budgeted**, a Petronas dividend of ~RM33.5bn vs RM20bn budgeted, and a deficit risk of 3.8% vs the 3.5% target — all built on a **1H2026 Brent average of US$92.50 against Budget 2026's US$65 assumption**. Brent at ~$84 (and falling) materially narrows that gap. Malaysia's exposure to the oil move is therefore primarily **fiscal, not trade** — the subsidy bill and the Petronas dividend, not the export line.
