# Commodities

## Thesis

The dominant cross-asset driver right now is a genuine supply shock, not a demand story: Houthi attacks on Saudi tankers plus renewed US strikes on Iran created what's being described as a 'two-chokepoint problem' (Bab el-Mandeb plus Strait of Hormuz risk), sending Brent up roughly 30% in a month toward $100/bbl. Gold, notably, fell rather than rallied — a sign the market is currently weighting the rate-hike implications of the shock above its safe-haven properties.

## Supporting Evidence

Brent ~$97-100/bbl intraday (23 Jul), vs. ~$71.57 on 1 Jul 2026 (~+30% m/m); WTI toward ~$90/bbl; gold (Aug futures) fell ~1.9% to ~$4,075-4,087 (23 Jul morning ET).

## Contradicting Evidence

Gold falling during a geopolitical escalation is itself a contradiction of the simple 'flight to safety' narrative — flagged explicitly as a cross-asset divergence.
