## 1. The Week That Was (20–24 Jul)

The week the oil shock met the AI-capex scare. Brent rose a third straight week (+~9–12% w/w, settling ~$97.4 Friday after closing above $100 Thursday) on the Houthi blockade of Saudi Arabia, tanker strikes and US–Iran hostilities; UST 10Y touched ~4.70% (highest since Jan 2025) and Bund 10Y 3.20% (15-year high) as markets moved to price hawkish central banks — including a live-ish July FOMC. Simultaneously, Alphabet's $195–205bn capex guide and Tesla's miss reframed AI spending as a margin question: US weekly scores — S&P 500 −0.6%, Dow −0.4%, Nasdaq −2.1%, Russell 2000 −1.1% (AP). The damage went global on Friday: Kospi −5.72% (sidecar halt; Samsung −7.6%, SK Hynix −8.3%), Nikkei −2.73%, TAIEX −2.67%. Europe diverged — SAP's beat and strong flash PMIs lifted the STOXX 600 into Friday's close. KLCI fell 1.76% on the week to 1,701.02; ringgit firmed to 4.0885/0935; CPO hit a 15-week high (RM4,723, Oct).

Data was uniformly growth-positive: EZ composite PMI 51.9 (cons. 50.5), UK 52.1 (cons. 49.7), US services 53.6 (cons. 51.5), US claims 187k, new home sales 628k (cons. 609k), UK retail sales +1.0% m/m (cons. −0.3%). Japan June core CPI 1.6% matched the Reuters poll. And US tariffs (10%/12.5% on ~60 partners, ~99% of imports) quietly took effect Friday — an *implemented* trade shock, not a threatened one.

## 2. Regime Change Assessment

Regime held at **risk-off / stagflationary-stress** all week, but the composition shifted: growth evidence *strengthened* (PMIs) while the inflation impulse stayed energy-led. The weekend added the first genuine de-escalation evidence (US–Iran mutual strike pause, Oman-mediated talks progressing per AP, Saudi coalition declaring its Houthi response "over") against a live counterpoint (Jizan refinery, 400k b/d, set ablaze Saturday). If the pause holds through the FOMC and Brent keeps retracing, the honest next label is **neutral-with-hawkish-tail** — but per desk rules a regime flip needs corroboration across ≥2 asset classes, not one weekend of headlines.
