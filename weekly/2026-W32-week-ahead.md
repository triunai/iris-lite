## 1. The week that was (W31: 27–31 July) — review of the completed week

W31 was the densest policy week of the cycle and it ended by breaking its own central assumption.

**Three central banks, three hawkish holds, three dissent blocs, inside 26 hours.** The Fed held 3.50–3.75% on 29 July **9–3** (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan dissenting *for a hike*); the BoE held 3.75% on 30 July **6–3** (Greene, Mann, Pill), with Bailey nonetheless leaning dovish on domestic conditions; the BOJ held 1.00% on 31 July **8–1** (Takata for 1.25%), revised growth up, and Ueda said the pace of hikes "could speed up." A synchronised global tightening *bias* without a single delivered hike.

**The data cut both ways.** US Q2 GDP advance **+1.5%** (a clear miss against ~2.3%); June core PCE **+0.1% m/m** (cool); then Q2 **ECI +0.9% q/q vs +0.8%** with real private wages −0.4% y/y (hot). Eurozone Q2 GDP **+0.4% q/q** beat with no technical recession, but July flash HICP came in at 2.9% headline / **core 2.5%** with **energy +10.0% y/y** — the war premium arriving inside official price data. China's NBS manufacturing PMI fell to **49.2**, a first contraction in five months. Malaysia's Q2 advance GDP printed **+5.8% y/y**, above BNM's own 4–5% band.

**The chip shock reversed in a single session.** After a −10.84% Kospi day on 28 July, Friday 31 July delivered **Kospi +17.91% — the largest single-day gain in KRX history** — plus **TAIEX +7.98%** (largest-ever point gain), SK Hynix +29.8% and TSMC +10% both limit-up, Nikkei +4%, and record foreign buying (~₩7.25–7.57tn / NT$67.6bn). Catalysts: MSFT +15% on Azure passing a $100bn run-rate and AMZN's AWS +37%, the fastest in 18 quarters.

**And the yen became a bilateral policy project.** Japan's MOF intervened ~¥8.2–8.45tn on 30 July (163.65 → sub-158). Then on 31 July **the US Treasury intervened itself** — the NY Fed selling euros to buy yen through Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, ~$5–10bn contemplated per Bessent's notepad. The first joint US–Japan intervention since 2011.

**Weekly closes.** S&P 500 +1.0%, Nasdaq Composite +1.6%, Dow +1.0%, Russell 2000 +0.05%. July: **S&P −0.13%, Dow +0.32% (fourth straight winning month), Nasdaq −3.20%, Russell −3.08%** — a month in which the index went nowhere and the composition changed completely. Brent +24% for July, WTI +21%; gold +0.65%, its first monthly gain in five; Henry Hub −17%. USDKRW **−8.81% for the won in July, its largest monthly gain since March 2009**; DXY −1.46%.

**Then the weekend broke the premise.** A prepared US/Israeli strike package on Iranian energy infrastructure was **cancelled** by Trump at 23:10 EDT Saturday; Iran–Oman Hormuz talks reached "final stages"; OPEC+ added +188k b/d for September, **completing** the 1.65m b/d voluntary unwind. Brent gapped ~4.65% lower on the Sunday reopen to ~$83.84.

## 2. How the regime changed

**Label: risk-off / stagflationary-stress — held, confidence cut to low-medium, resolving tonight.** The 31 July three-condition downgrade gate passed 2 of 3; the third (Korea/Taiwan holding >50% of Friday's gain) is untestable until today's Asian close.

The substantive change is compositional, and it is the most important analytical shift this desk has made since it opened:

| Axis | W30 → W31 | Where it stands now |
|---|---|---|
| **Energy / war** | The stress driver | **Broken.** Brent from a $100 test to ~$84 in nine sessions, *through* the war's widest escalation; OPEC+ unwind complete; strike cancelled |
| **Rates / curve** | An unnamed side-effect | **Now the primary stressor.** 30Y closed **5.274%**, a *new* 2007-era high, on the same session VIX closed 15.99 |
| **AI-capex / chip** | Outside the framework | **Resolved violently upward in Asia, unconfirmed in the US** (SOX +0.07% Friday after +8.19%) |

Oil is no longer what is stressing this regime. The long end is. That single sentence is what drove this week's scenario restructure.

## 3. Scenarios — which gained and which lost

The board was **restructured** on the Monday run, as flagged since 28 July and deliberately deferred from the 31 July recovery run.

- **Base — renamed `curve-led-squeeze-war-premium-deflating`, band HELD at 50–60%.** The oil leg resolved *toward* this scenario (a range that held rather than a $100 breakout or a crash); the rates leg tightened *inside* it. The base case is now stressing itself internally.
- **Upside — renamed `de-escalation-and-disinflation-relief`, band RAISED 10–20% → 15–25%.** Four confirmation signals fired in one weekend — the strike cancellation, Hormuz talks in "final stages," the ~5% crude gap, and the completed OPEC+ unwind — three of them confirmed facts rather than interpretation. The raise is capped at 25% for one specific reason: **Iran denies the framing.** Mehr called Trump's account "a new lie," and the Persian Gulf Strait Authority still refuses transit permits.
- **Downside — renamed `stagflationary-policy-shock` → `curve-led-policy-shock`, band HELD at 20–30% but RE-SPECIFIED.** The old trigger (Brent >$100 sustained + a delivered hike) has half-collapsed. The new trigger is a **September hike delivered onto a +1.5%-GDP economy with the 30Y above 5.5%**. Falling oil does not invalidate this scenario — it changes which market transmits it, and arguably removes the excuse for treating a hike as temporary.
- **New cross-cutting axis: `ai-capex-and-chip-cycle`.** Recorded as a modifier to all three rather than a fourth scenario, closing the coverage gap carried since 29 July.

## 6. Unresolved themes carried forward

- **The Hormuz gap.** A −5% oil price against a strait that is formally still shut and an arrangement Iran has not ratified. Watch for Iranian confirmation, transit counts recovering toward 60+/week (last sourced: 10 vessels on 23 July vs an ~88/day baseline), and whether Trump's announced Monday talks actually convene.
- **The VIX/30Y divergence.** Now in its third week and *widening*: VIX 15.99 against a 30Y at a new 2007-era high. One of these markets is wrong.
- **Russian product cracks.** Ufa (>23mt/yr), Rosneft's Saratov (~7mt/yr), Nizhnekamsk and Ryazan all impaired with runs at 2005 lows *while crude falls*. Diesel is where the two supply chains collide, and no diesel-crack print has been sourced.
