## 1. The 90-Second Plain-English Read

Markets got a lot less nervous on Monday. Oil — which had spiked to over $100 a barrel the week before on fears that the US and Iran were heading toward a wider war — fell 8.7% in a single day, its biggest drop in three months, after the two sides held their fire for a second straight night and back-channel talks in Oman reportedly made progress. That single event rippled through nearly everything: bond yields eased, the odds of an emergency US interest-rate hike this week fell, and stock markets from Frankfurt to Seoul to Kuala Lumpur rallied.

But two things didn't fit the "everyone's relieved" story, and they're the most useful part of today's read. First, gold didn't fall the way you'd expect if the scare were really over — it held near $4,100, meaning investors kept their insurance policies rather than cashing them in. Second, the Japanese yen hit a fresh 40-year low the same day oil crashed, even though cheaper oil should be *good* news for Japan (it imports almost all its energy) — something else, not the oil story, is driving the yen right now, and the honest answer is we don't fully know what yet (positioning and pre-central-bank-meeting jitters are the leading suspects, evidenced below).

The next two days matter more than Monday did: Israel's prime minister meets Trump at the White House tonight with Iran "first and foremost" on the agenda, and the US Federal Reserve delivers its rate decision Thursday morning (Malaysia time). If the ceasefire holds through both, the "the world is on edge" reading built up over the past two weeks starts to look overdone. If either breaks, Monday's rally reverses fast.

## 2. Relationship & Ratio Dashboard

*New standing section — full pair definitions in `config/ratio-pairs.yaml`, daily levels logged in `trackers/relationship-dashboard.csv`. Per the desk's statistical-honesty rule, percentile/z-score/correlation figures say `insufficient history` rather than a guessed number — this tracker starts accumulating its own history today, so future digests will carry real 1-week and 1-month changes.*

**Oil vs. gold — the shock is fading, the fear isn't.** Brent fell to $88.36 (settlement, −8.7%) while gold held near $4,074–4,098 — a ratio of roughly 0.0216, down about 10% from Friday's ~0.0241. Plain-English: oil is cheapening much faster than gold, which tells you the market is unwinding a *specific* supply-shock premium (Middle East tankers, refineries) while keeping its broader macro/geopolitical hedges in place. *Confidence: medium — gold quoted a range, not one clean settlement.*

**Brent vs. WTI — the risk is still more "global" than "American."** The spread narrowed to $5.75 (Brent $88.36 − WTI $82.61) from about $7.61 Friday. Brent carries more of the Hormuz/Red Sea risk premium than WTI does, so a narrowing spread says that specific global-shipping-risk premium eased faster than the general oil-demand story. *Confidence: medium.*

**Copper vs. gold — a small tilt toward growth-optimism, with an honest caveat.** Using COMEX copper (635¢/lb) against gold, the ratio ticked up roughly 1.8% from Friday's LME-based reading — but the venue changed (LME-per-tonne on Friday vs. COMEX-per-lb on Monday), so this is a directional read only, flagged as such in the tracker, not a precise like-for-like number. It's *consistent* with Monday's broad equity rebound (DAX, Kospi, Shanghai, KLCI all up) but shouldn't be over-weighted on its own.

**Gold vs. silver — first clean reading, no history yet.** Ratio ~70 (gold ~$4,086 midpoint ÷ silver $58.38). No Friday comparison exists (silver wasn't cleanly sourced that day), so this is a baseline for future comparison, not a signal yet.

**Copper vs. aluminium — first reading.** ~4.42 (COMEX copper converted to ~$14,000/tonne ÷ aluminium $3,168.35/tonne, Trading Economics). A baseline; the venue mismatch (COMEX vs. LME-referenced) is flagged in the tracker.

**Palm oil vs. soybean oil — a real, sourced competitiveness read.** Converting Monday's early-trade CPO print (RM4,683/tonne ÷ USDMYR 4.0835 ≈ $1,147/tonne) against CBOT soybean oil's Monday settlement (71.46¢/lb ≈ $1,576/tonne) gives CPO trading at roughly a **27% discount** to soybean oil. That discount is normally supportive of Malaysian and Indonesian export competitiveness into price-sensitive cooking-oil and biodiesel demand. Caveat: the CPO leg is an early-trade quote, not Monday's settlement (unavailable this run) — a genuine gap to close next run.

**Wheat vs. corn — level only.** 1.461 (wheat $6.60/bu ÷ corn $4.5175/bu, CBOT Monday closes). A rising ratio generally nudges livestock-feed formulation toward more corn; no historical threshold is asserted without a source.

**Gas benchmarks — the regional gap dwarfs Monday's move.** Converting Monday's closes to a common unit: Dutch TTF (Europe) ≈ **$18.94/MMBtu** (€56.85/MWh, −9.96% on the day per Trading Economics/XTB) versus US Henry Hub **$2.77/MMBtu** (−3.6%, Rigzone/Bloomberg settlement) — Europe is still paying roughly **6.8× the US price** even after its own 10% Monday drop. Asia's JKM benchmark was quoted in the high-$20s/MMBtu, but that print is 10 days stale (17 Jul, Global LNG Hub) — included only to show the scale of the regional gap, not as a same-day data point.

**Credit vs. equity volatility — a genuine divergence worth sitting with.** US high-yield spreads sat at 2.79% (279bp) as of Friday's latest FRED print — a historically tight, calm level — while VIX showed at least some intraday elevation Monday (a conflicting 17.7–19.4 range, VIX's official close unavailable this run). Plain-English: bond investors who lend to riskier companies showed essentially *no* extra concern through the entire two-week oil/Iran episode, while stock-market volatility ticked up. That's consistent with credit reading the shock as transitory rather than a fundamental repricing of risk — though it's Analyst inference, not confirmed by a source stating that reasoning.

**Copper vs. the yuan — an agreement, for contrast.** Copper firmed (+0.7%) alongside the yuan strengthening to its best level since mid-June (USD/CNY 6.7670) — both point the same way (China-demand-supportive), unlike most of the divergences flagged above. Worth naming explicitly so "divergence" doesn't become the only story told.

**USD/JPY vs. the US–Japan yield gap — today's sharpest divergence.** The core carry-trade driver — the US 10Y minus Japan 10Y yield gap — was essentially flat (~1.88 percentage points Monday vs. ~1.87pp Friday, roughly +1bp). Yet the yen hit a **fresh 40-year intraday low of 163.99** the same day. When the textbook driver barely moves but the currency hits a new extreme anyway, something else is doing the work — in this case, most likely CFTC-tracked speculative yen shorts already sitting at −152,125 contracts and still building (see §5), plus positioning ahead of Friday's BOJ decision. *Confidence: medium-high on the data, Analyst inference on the causal read.*

## 3. Country Winners / Losers

*New standing section — full exposure map in `config/country-commodity-exposure.yaml`. A country only appears below if that day's FX or equity move was independently sourced; the map says what to check, never what happened.*

**Clean agreements (moved the way their commodity exposure predicts):**

- **India** — a large net oil *importer*. Crude's collapse was the Confirmed catalyst for both Sensex/Nifty's rally (+1.0%, snapping a 5-day losing streak) *and* the rupee's best session in six-plus weeks (95.90, from 96.53 Friday). Equities and FX agreed — the textbook case.
- **China** — a large net oil importer at the margin. Equities rallied (Shanghai +1.15%, Shenzhen +2.72%) and the yuan hit its strongest level since mid-June, both consistent with cheaper energy input costs plus Politburo-stimulus hopes. (June industrial profit growth, released the same day, was already slowing — +15.1% y/y vs. +21.1% in May — a reminder that not every input works in the exporter's favor at once.)

**Genuine divergences (didn't move the way the map would predict — flagged, not asserted as fact):**

- **Japan** — a near-total net energy importer; cheaper oil and LNG should be an unambiguous tailwind. Instead, the yen hit a fresh 40-year low the same day (§2's sharpest divergence), and the Nikkei's own gain (+0.50%) was modest relative to the size of the oil move, with the rally concentrated in domestic-demand names (TOPIX +1.37%) while AI/semiconductor stocks were sold off. Read: positioning and pre-BOJ jitters currently swamp Japan's terms-of-trade improvement.
- **South Korea** — also a net energy importer. Kospi rose 0.97%, but the won *weakened* (1,468.6, +6.1) on foreign investors net-selling ₩2.885 trillion of Korean equities the same day. Equities up, currency down: foreign portfolio outflows are dominating over both the energy-import tailwind and the equity rebound itself.

**Two-sided by design (the country-exposure map itself predicts a mixed signal):**

- **Malaysia** — Petronas-linked oil/LNG export revenue takes a hit from the Brent collapse (Bursa's energy sub-index fell), while the RM40–50bn/year fuel-subsidy bill gets relief — the two effects run in opposite directions, which is exactly what the exposure map notes for Malaysia. The KLCI still rose 0.71% (bank-led, on negative breadth), and the ringgit firmed — but that looks driven more by pre-FOMC positioning and bargain-hunting in financials than by the oil move specifically.
- **Gulf exporters (bloc)** — oil's collapse is fiscally negative against these economies' spending plans, but the *cause* (US–Iran de-escalation) is unambiguously positive for regional security. No FX confirmation is available or expected — most peg to the US dollar, which mechanically insulates the exchange rate from this kind of test.

**Flagged, not called (no data to confirm either way — genuine gaps, not omissions):**

- **Russia** — a clear commodity-exposure loser from the oil-price collapse, compounded the same day by Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil export/refining infrastructure (Rostov terminal, Yaroslavl, Udmurtia). No RUB or Russian-equity data was sourced to confirm a market reaction — logged as a mechanism (Analyst inference), not an observed fact.
- **Norway, Canada** (oil exporters), **Australia** (iron ore/coal — no benchmark-specific move sourced today, distinct from the Dalian *domestic Chinese* iron-ore contract that was checked), **Chile/Peru** (copper firmed mildly, +0.7%, but CLP/PEN weren't sourced), **Indonesia** (CPO's discount to soybean oil is relevant to it too, but IDR/JCI weren't checked this run) — all genuine gaps for the Tuesday Asia-Pacific / Thursday Middle-East-Africa rotation slots (§7.7) to close on subsequent runs, rather than forced calls today.

## 7. Political Reaction-Function Board

*New standing section (`trackers/political-reaction-function.csv`) — generalizes the existing "TACO pattern" tracking (§7.4) beyond US tariffs. Uses qualitative bands (low/medium/high) plus a direction (rising/falling/stable), never a fabricated numeric score — a made-up "7 out of 10" would imply a measurement method that doesn't exist and would itself be invented data.*

**US administration — Iran military-strike campaign: pressure band medium-high, direction falling (from high).**

Evidence, each independently sourced: Brent's spike above $100 before the pause carried a real political and inflation cost, and Fed hike odds had risen to 38% partly on that oil shock; multiple approval polls published in the 8–24 July window (CBS/YouGov 39/61, Emerson 39/57, Washington Post/Ipsos 37/61, CNBC 40/59; aggregated 36.8% approve / 59.8% disapprove per FiftyPlusOne, 27 Jul) show approval sliding into the high-30s, with Forbes' 27 July framing tying the decline explicitly to war fatigue; CBS separately reported officials worried about rapidly expending munitions and interceptor stockpiles and about Indo-Pacific military readiness. An **off-ramp is visible**: Oman's mediation gives both sides a face-saving way to frame a pause as diplomatic progress rather than retreat, rather than requiring either side to look like it backed down unilaterally.

This is descriptive, not predictive — it says the pressure to de-escalate is real and building, not that de-escalation is guaranteed. **Invalidation:** a resumption of strikes despite the talks, or an attack at Jizan's scale attributed to the other side, would say the pressure identified here did not actually change the trajectory.

**OPEC+ — oil-price defense vs. quota discipline: pressure band medium, direction stable.**

OPEC+ is set to raise nominal quotas by roughly 188,000 b/d for a fourth straight month (per OilPrice.com's 23 July preview) even though actual June output (36.28 million b/d) already runs well below quota — a messaging move more than a real supply response, since physical damage (the Jizan fire, the halted Saudi west-coast loadings) constrains how much more oil members could offer regardless of the quota level. Monday's 8.7% price drop also reduces the immediate pressure to defend price through output discipline. **Watch:** the 2 August meeting — an actual *cut* (not just a quota hold or symbolic raise) would be the clearest sign real price-defense pressure has built.
