## 5. Supply-Chain Pulse

Two chains moved in opposite directions. **(1) Hormuz — the physical position has NOT improved even as the price says it has.** Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority is still refusing transit permits; CENTCOM has redirected **35 commercial vessels and disabled two** since the 14 July blockade began (up from 9 and 1 on 22 July); the LNG carrier **Gaslog Shanghai**, carrying a Qatari cargo, was **struck by a projectile on 31 July** near the strait's western entrance and went AIS-dark — unclaimed, and it came one day after Qatar's first resumed transit in three weeks. Hormuz carries roughly a fifth of global LNG. The reported deal architecture — ships entering via the Iranian side and exiting through Oman, with a 60-day toll-free period — is credible but unsigned. **The gap between a −5% oil price and an unchanged closed strait is the single most important thing on this desk today** (Analyst inference, high confidence). **(2) Russian refining — sharply worse.** In 48 hours Ukraine struck the **Ufa/Bashneft cluster (>23mt/yr combined)**, **Rosneft's Saratov refinery (~7mt/yr)**, petrochemical facilities at Nizhnekamsk, the Lyudinovo oil depot and the **Engels strategic bomber base**; it also sank the sanctioned Russian container ship *Yanina* near Novorossiysk. Russia claims 635 drones downed. With Russian runs already at 2005 lows, the diesel/product-crack channel tightens even as crude falls — **these two chains now point opposite ways, and product cracks are where they collide.** Black Sea grain corridor: still fully halted.
