## 5. Supply-Chain Pulse

Material new evidence, from the ISM's own commentary and sub-indices — the best-sourced supply-chain read the desk has had in a week.

**Energy → producer prices.** ISM **prices paid fell 73.0 → 71.1** — a *deceleration from a very high base*, not deflation; at 71.1 a large majority of purchasing managers were still paying more. Respondents named **pricing volatility in 57% of negative comments** and **the Iran conflict in 43%** (fuel, freight, commodity availability), against **tariffs at only 18%**. The July print therefore already contains the war; the **August** print is where the barrel's collapse should first show.

**Logistics → delivery times → inventories.** Supplier deliveries at **58.9 slowed for an eighth month** (higher = slower), but ISM reads this as demand-driven, and the sub-indices agree: production +6.3, backlog 50.5 → 55.0, imports 52.9 → 55.7. That is a factory sector filling orders faster than suppliers can feed it, not one waiting on blocked chokepoints. Customers' inventories were **"too low" at 40.7%** against a rising backlog — the configuration that pulls production forward (*inference*), and the mechanism by which a hot July ISM could keep the goods economy running into the autumn, which is precisely what the curve thesis fears.

**The unresolved link.** Russian refinery outages (Ufa, Saratov, Nizhnekamsk, Ryazan at 2005-low runs) remain where falling crude and constrained product supply collide, and **no diesel-crack print was sourced for a second consecutive run**. Until one is, the "cheaper crude means cheaper fuel" chain has an unverified link in it.
